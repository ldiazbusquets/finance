01 · The business

CPUs it leads in, GPUs it is catching up in.

AMD has four businesses. Data Center (58% of revenue) sells EPYC server processors, where it has taken share from Intel for years, and Instinct AI accelerators. Client sells Ryzen PC chips, Gaming the chips inside the PlayStation and Xbox, and Embedded the Xilinx programmable chips it bought in 2022.

The investment case is AI. With the MI450 series and the Helios rack, AMD sells a whole AI system rather than single chips. OpenAI agreed in October 2025 to deploy 6 gigawatts of AMD GPUs and received a warrant for up to 160 million AMD shares that vests as deployments and the share price hit milestones. In 2026 Anthropic agreed to deploy up to 2 gigawatts of MI450 GPUs in Helios racks, and Meta, Microsoft and Oracle are customers too.

Second-quarter revenue rose 50% and Data Center more than doubled. Gaming fell 31% and Embedded grew 19%. GAAP operating income was $2.0 billion and free cash flow $1.56 billion, 14% of revenue. The shares fell 7% the next day because investors wanted more.

For the third quarter AMD guided to about $13.0 billion of revenue and a 56% gross margin. Lisa Su described Q3 as the start of the Helios ramp, Q4 as a step up and Q1 2027 as a further step up, and management expects Data Center revenue to more than double again in 2027. The stock closed at a record $649.42 on October 6 and has fallen about 15% since. Results come on November 3.

04 · Balance sheet and ownership

About $10 billion of net cash, and a warrant to OpenAI.

At the end of June AMD had $13.1 billion of cash and short-term investments and $3.2 billion of debt. The OpenAI warrant is the unusual item: up to 160 million shares, about 10% of today’s count, for a nominal price, with the last tranche tied to a $600 share price. This model counts all of it, so shares rise from 1.63 billion to about 1.80 billion by 2030.

05 · Valuation

Our scenarios give $319 a share.

2030 revenue and operating margin, taxed at 21%, times an exit multiple, discounted back four years at 12% (beta 1.6 gives a CAPM cost of about 12.3%), plus net cash. AMD’s own long-term targets, set in November 2025, call for revenue growth above 35% a year and earnings above $20 a share; it now says it expects to beat that figure significantly.

07 · Risks

What could go wrong, and what could go right.

RiskWhy it matters
Helios timingGrowth after Q3 depends on racks shipping on schedule; a slip would hit 2027.
NvidiaNvidia still has most of the market, a software lead (CUDA) and a yearly product cadence.
Customer concentrationA few AI labs and cloud providers account for most of the GPU growth.
AI spending cycleIf AI data center spending slows after 2027, the highest-growth part of AMD slows with it.
Dilution and export rulesThe OpenAI warrant adds up to 10% more shares; China export limits already cost $800 million in 2025.

Upside: a faster Helios ramp, share gains in AI inference, more lab deals like Anthropic’s, and continued EPYC gains in servers.

08 · Recommendation

Watch. The right products, a price ahead of them.

AMD is in the best position in its history: leading in server CPUs and finally a credible second supplier of AI systems, with signed demand from OpenAI and Anthropic. At $552.72, though, the price is close to our bull case and about 60% above the weighted value of $340. Third-quarter results on November 3 and the size of the fourth-quarter step-up are the next test; a price nearer $350–400, or proof that 2027 data center revenue is running well ahead of plan, would make it a Buy.

Final view

A real challenger to Nvidia, already priced as one.

The products and customers are there; the shares have run ahead of the revenue.
