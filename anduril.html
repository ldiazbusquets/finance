01 · The business

Weapons built like tech products.

Anduril was founded in 2017 by Palmer Luckey, Trae Stephens, Brian Schimpf (now CEO) and others. It designs and builds its own products and sells them, rather than waiting for the Pentagon to fund development. Its range includes the Fury collaborative combat aircraft, Roadrunner reusable interceptors, Barracuda low-cost cruise missiles, Ghost Shark underwater drones and surveillance towers, all linked by Lattice, its AI command software.

Production is moving to Arsenal-1, a plant of nearly $1 billion in Ohio designed to build up to 150 Fury aircraft a year.

04 · Funding and ownership

$14 billion to $90 billion in two years.

Anduril was valued at $14 billion in August 2024, $30.5 billion in June 2025 and $61 billion in May 2026, when it raised $5 billion led by Thrive Capital and Andreessen Horowitz. An employee share sale that opened on October 13 values it at $90 billion ($94.80 a share). Talks about a round near $100 billion were reported in July but not confirmed. No S-1 has been filed.

Private investors are paying for growth well beyond 2030, and for the chance that Anduril becomes a new prime contractor with software margins. Traditional defense primes such as L3Harris trade at a fraction of that multiple.

08 · Our view

A great company priced for perfection.

Anduril is growing like a top software company, but our scenarios value it far below the $61–90 billion private marks. For the plan to buy on IPO day, the numbers argue for patience: early public prices are likely to start from those marks, and the base case does not support them. L3Harris, rated Buy in its own report, offers defense exposure at a much lower price in the meantime.

Final view

Watch it, don’t chase it.

The business is excellent; the valuation already assumes it stays that way for a decade.
