01 · The business

Claude, sold mostly to businesses.

Anthropic was founded in 2021 by Dario and Daniela Amodei and other former OpenAI researchers. It sells access to its Claude models through its own apps and API and through Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud. Most revenue comes from businesses and developers rather than consumers, led by coding: Claude Code has become one of its largest products.

It is a public benefit corporation, and an independent Long-Term Benefit Trust holds a special class of shares with the right to elect directors, a structure meant to keep safety ahead of short-term profit.

Revenue has grown faster than at almost any company of its size. Third-quarter revenue of $16.8 billion, reported by Bloomberg and the Financial Times from investor presentations, was up 45% from the second quarter, but some investors noted that monthly growth is slowing. OpenAI has argued that Anthropic counts sales through cloud partners at their gross value, which flatters the comparison; the public S-1 will settle how revenue is reported.

Anthropic reached an adjusted operating profit in the second quarter, rare for an AI lab, but it has committed to more than half a trillion dollars of computing with Amazon, Google, SpaceX and others. Those contracts are the main risk if growth slows. None of these figures is audited yet; the public S-1 will be the first full set.

04 · Funding and ownership

Valued at $965 billion in May.

The last private round was a $65 billion Series H at a $965 billion valuation in May 2026. Amazon owns an estimated 15–21% (non-voting) and Google about 14%. A confidential S-1 was submitted on June 1; the listing is expected on Nasdaq in mid-November, led by Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Citi. Investors have discussed valuations from about $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion.

07 · Risks

What could go wrong, and what could go right.

Upside: AI agents and coding tools growing into a large share of software spending, with margins rising as computing gets cheaper.

08 · Our view

The fastest-growing software company, at an unknown IPO price.

The base case supports roughly the May private valuation, about $1 trillion. A listing at $1.5–2 trillion would price in close to the bull case on its first day.

What would change the value: The prospectus’s margins, compute commitments and the IPO price are the numbers that decide whether the shares are worth buying.
