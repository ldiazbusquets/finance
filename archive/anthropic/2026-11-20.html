01 · The business

Claude, sold mostly to businesses.

Anthropic was founded in 2021 by Dario and Daniela Amodei and other former OpenAI researchers. It sells access to its Claude models through its own apps and API and through Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud. Most revenue comes from businesses and developers rather than consumers, led by coding: Claude Code has become one of its largest products.

It is a public benefit corporation, and an independent Long-Term Benefit Trust holds a special class of shares with the right to elect directors, a structure meant to keep safety ahead of short-term profit.

Revenue has grown faster than at almost any company of its size. The public S-1 confirmed third-quarter revenue of $17.0 billion, more than twelve times a year earlier and about 45% above the second quarter, and $33.2 billion for the first nine months of 2026, against $2.5 billion a year before. Some investors noted that monthly growth is slowing. About 44% of revenue is sold through the AWS, Google Cloud and Azure marketplaces; OpenAI has argued that Anthropic counts those sales at their gross value, which flatters the comparison. No single customer was more than 10% of revenue, and the top five were about a quarter.

Anthropic made an operating profit in the second quarter, rare for an AI lab, and slipped back to a small loss in the third as it added computing. The huge net loss is mostly an accounting charge on convertible notes and other instruments whose value rose with the company’s. The real risk is the commitments: the S-1 lists about $900 billion of computing contracts with Broadcom, Google, SpaceX, Amazon, Microsoft and others, up from about $518 billion reported in October, and about $452 billion of it falls due before 2031. Those contracts must be paid even if growth slows.

04 · Funding and ownership

Priced at $83, about $1.5 trillion.

The last private round was a $65 billion Series H at a $965 billion valuation in May 2026. Amazon owns an estimated 15–21% (non-voting) and Google about 14%.

On November 18 Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share, above the $70–78 range set the week before, with orders reported at about seven times the shares on offer. It sold 980 million Class A shares, up from 825 million: about 956 million new shares and about 24 million from the co-founders. That raised about $81.3 billion, the largest IPO on record, ahead of SpaceX’s roughly $75 billion in June; about $79 billion goes to the company. Underwriters led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley can buy up to 147 million more shares within 30 days, which would take the deal to about $93.5 billion. Seven tech companies, including Google and Nvidia, also agreed to buy $26 billion of non-voting Class N shares at the IPO price.

At $83 the company is valued at about $1.5 trillion fully diluted, or about $1.1 trillion on the shares outstanding, roughly 55% above the May round. The shares are listed on the Nasdaq Global Select Market as ANT. Up to 315 million shares held by insiders other than the founders can be sold from the first day; most other holders are locked up for up to 180 days, and the co-founders and some large holders for up to a year. A single Class F share gives the co-founders 50.1% of the votes, and the Long-Term Benefit Trust elects a majority of the board.

On its first day, November 19, ANT opened at $111.50, 34% above the IPO price and briefly about $2 trillion fully diluted, then gave back much of the gain to close at $97.17, up 17%. That values the company at about $1.75 trillion fully diluted, or about $1.27 trillion on the shares outstanding. The debut did not lift the wider market: the major US indexes fell that day amid worries about AI valuations and infrastructure costs.

Dilution: What Today’s Owners Keep

The values above are for the whole company in 2030. A private company that is still burning cash pays for it by selling new shares, and the money those buyers put in is theirs, not today’s owners’. So the value of a stake bought today is the whole-company value less the present value of the capital still to be raised. The table shows that for several amounts raised evenly over the next four years, discounted at the model’s 15%. “Share sold” is how much of the company those raises would buy at the last valuation of $1,750B; at a lower price, the share sold would be larger.

08 · Our view

The fastest-growing software company, trading beyond the bull case.

The base case supports roughly the May private valuation, about $1 trillion, or about $1.07 trillion with the IPO cash. The IPO priced at $83 a share, about $1.5 trillion fully diluted, close to the bull case; it closed its first day at $97.17, about $1.75 trillion, beyond it. An entry near $1–1.2 trillion, roughly $55–66 a share, would leave room for things to go only moderately right.

What would change the value: Revenue growth in the first quarterly reports as a public company, progress on margins, and whether about $900 billion of compute commitments stay matched by demand. Now that ANT trades, the report could move to share-price coverage.
