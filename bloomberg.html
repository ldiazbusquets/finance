01 · The business

The Terminal, and everything around it.

Founded in 1981 to sell bond prices to traders, Bloomberg LP now makes most of its money from the Terminal, which costs about $30,000 a year per user and is found on almost every trading desk. Around it sit enterprise data feeds, indices, trading and risk software, and Bloomberg Media: Bloomberg News (about 2,700 journalists), Businessweek, television, radio and Bloomberg.com.

The pieces reinforce each other: the news makes the Terminal more useful, and Terminal revenue pays for the newsroom. Decades of proprietary prices and articles are also valuable training data for AI models, which is one reason an AI lab or exchange could want a stake.

04 · Funding and ownership

Owned 88% by its founder.

Michael Bloomberg, now 84, owns an estimated 88% and has said the company will eventually pass to Bloomberg Philanthropies. He recently moved some shares to the foundation, which would reduce taxes if the company listed or sold a stake. In 2008 Bloomberg bought back Merrill Lynch’s 20% stake for $4.4 billion, which valued the whole company at about $22 billion.

In July 2026 Semafor reported early, informal talks with bankers about an IPO or another transaction. The company replied that “Mike controls the firm” and has no plans to sell. There is no filing, date or exchange. A minority stake sold to an AI lab, exchange or asset manager is as likely as a public listing, and might never reach retail investors.

08 · Our view

The Coca-Cola of financial data, if it ever lists.

Bloomberg is dominant, recurring and slow-changing. Near the $80 billion bankers mention, the base case needs a multiple close to the best data companies: fair but not cheap; much above $100 billion it would assume the bull case.

What would change the value: A prospectus with audited margins and Terminal renewal rates would replace the estimates this report relies on.
