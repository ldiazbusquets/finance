Bonds, priced and shocked

Price any fixed-coupon bond, see its duration and convexity, then hit a portfolio with a rate move. Yields start at today’s Treasury curve.

MOD-07

Bond pricer

Enter a yield to get the price, or a price to get the yield. Everything else follows from the cash flows.

Price against yield

The copper curve is the real price. The dashed line is what duration alone predicts; the gap between them is convexity, and it always works in the holder’s favor.

Where the value comes from

Today’s value of each payment: coupons in copper, the face value at the end in grey. Duration is the average date, weighted by value.

Rate shock

Build a bond portfolio, then move the curve. The short and long ends can move separately, so you can test twists as well as parallel shifts.

Your bonds

Edit any cell. Coupons are paid twice a year.

The shock

Moves between 2 and 30 years are filled in on a straight line.

Gain or loss by size of move

Parallel shifts from −300 to +500 bp. Dashed: duration alone.

Prices assume settlement on a coupon date, so there is no accrued interest. Yields are compounded at the coupon frequency. For education only, not investment advice.
