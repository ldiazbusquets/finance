01 · The business

Oil, gas, refining and chemicals.

Upstream produces about 4.5 million barrels of oil and gas a day, more than 1.8 million of it in the Permian Basin after the 2024 purchase of Pioneer, and a fast-growing share from Guyana. Energy Products refines crude into fuels; Chemical Products and Specialty Products make plastics, lubricants and other materials.

Upstream earns most of the profit and moves with oil and gas prices; refining moves with fuel margins, which often rise when crude supply is disrupted.

Second-quarter earnings were $14.5 billion ($3.48 a share) on higher oil prices and record Permian output, with free cash flow of $17.2 billion and $9.4 billion returned to shareholders. Exxon plans Permian growth of about 9% a year through 2030, and a fifth Guyana production vessel should start in the fourth quarter, adding 250,000 barrels a day of capacity.

Its third-quarter guide points to much higher refining margins (+$4.5 billion) but lower oil realizations (−$1.3 billion) than the second quarter. Brent has since jumped to about $110 after Houthi attacks on Saudi targets. Results come on October 30.

04 · Balance sheet and ownership

Low debt, steady payouts.

At June 30 ExxonMobil had about $42 billion of debt and $11 billion of cash, about $32 billion of net debt: very low for a company of its size. It pays $1.03 a share each quarter and bought back $5.1 billion of stock in the second quarter.

08 · Rating

Sell. A great oil company, priced for a spike.

ExxonMobil has low debt, record Permian output and Guyana growing, but the price needs today’s spike in oil prices to last. On 2025’s margins, with Brent near $69, the value is about half the price.

What would change the value: Third-quarter results on October 30 and whatever happens to Brent, now near $110 on Middle East attacks, move the value most.
