01 · The business

Rockets, moon landers and missile defense.

Firefly, based in Cedar Park, Texas, has four product lines. Alpha is a small rocket that carries about a ton to low orbit. Blue Ghost is a lunar lander: in March 2025 it made the first fully successful commercial landing on the Moon. Elytra is a spacecraft that can serve as a satellite or carry payloads around the Moon. Eclipse, a larger rocket built with Northrop Grumman, is due to fly no earlier than 2027. Its SciTec division adds defense software for missile warning, which gives Firefly a role in the Golden Dome missile-defense program.

Firefly went public in August 2025 at $45 a share, raising $868 million at a valuation of about $10 billion.

Alpha’s record is the weak point: a failure in April 2025 and an explosion during a ground test in September 2025 grounded it until a successful flight in March 2026. Launch is what would lift margins, so the cadence of Alpha flights matters more than any single contract.

04 · Balance sheet and ownership

Enough cash, with dilution along the way.

Firefly had $635 million of cash and an undrawn $305 million credit line in June. It has sold shares twice since the IPO, including an offering at $48 in May 2026, and registered shares for resale by former SciTec owners, which has weighed on the price. Expect more share sales before it turns profitable. AE Industrial Partners is the largest investor; Northrop Grumman invested $50 million alongside the Eclipse partnership.

Buy. A real business, a risky stock.
