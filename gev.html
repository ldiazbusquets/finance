01 · The business

The machines that make electricity, and the grid that carries it.

GE Vernova is the former energy business of General Electric, spun off as its own company in April 2024. It sells equipment and, more importantly, decades of service on that equipment. Once a gas turbine or a grid substation is installed, it needs parts, upgrades and maintenance for 30 years or more, so every machine sold today is also a long service contract.

Demand changed in 2024 and 2025. Power use in the U.S. had been flat for about fifteen years; AI data centers, factories moving back onshore and the electrification of heating and transport have pushed it up again. Utilities need new gas plants that can run around the clock, and the grid needs transformers and switchgear to connect them. GE Vernova sells both.

Power

Gas turbines, the largest piece, plus steam, hydro and nuclear, including the BWRX-300 small modular reactor. Roughly half of revenue, and most of the profit.

Electrification

Grid equipment: transformers, switchgear, high-voltage links and the software that runs them. In February 2026 it bought the remaining half of Prolec GE, a transformer maker, for $5.275 billion.

Wind

Onshore and offshore turbines. Revenue is shrinking and the segment loses money, held back by U.S. permitting, tariffs and offshore project problems.

Who it competes with

In gas turbines, essentially two rivals: Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries. In grid equipment: Siemens Energy, Hitachi Energy, Schneider Electric and Eaton. In wind: Vestas and Siemens Gamesa.

Takeaway. A three-player market for large gas turbines, sold out for years, with a long tail of service revenue behind every sale. Few industrial businesses have that much visibility.

02 · The order book

$176 billion of backlog, and turbines sold into 2031.

The backlog is the reason the stock has re-rated. It reached a record $176 billion in June, up $13 billion in one quarter and from $129 billion a year earlier. Second-quarter orders were $24.2 billion, 88% higher than a year before, and Power orders alone more than doubled.

In gas turbines GE Vernova counts two kinds of commitment: firm orders (53 GW in June) and slot reservations, where a customer pays to hold a production slot (63 GW). Together that is 116 GW, and the company expects to end 2026 above 125 GW. For scale, it plans to build 20 GW a year from the third quarter of 2026, 24 GW in 2028 and 30 GW by 2030, so the factories are effectively sold out for about five years. Management said it is already taking reservations for 2031.

A sold-out factory sets its own prices. Management said orders taken in the first half of 2026 were priced more than 20% above the fourth-quarter 2025 average, and those higher prices reach revenue only as the turbines ship, from 2027 onward.

Read. The order book makes revenue for 2027 and 2028 unusually predictable. The open questions are margins and how long the cycle lasts, not demand.

Power is the profit engine. Its margin rose about 3 points in a year on an organic basis as pricing and volume improved, and service on the installed fleet keeps earnings steady between equipment cycles. Electrification is growing fastest: Prolec GE added about $5 billion of backlog, and data-center orders in the first half were more than double those for all of 2025. Both segments target a 22% margin by 2028.

Wind is the drag. Orders fell 40%, the segment lost $275 million in the quarter, and the 2028 plan has its revenue still falling. Because Wind is shrinking while the other two grow, the company margin rises even if nothing else changes.

Read. The 20% company margin target is mostly a mix story: more Power and Electrification at 22%, less Wind.

Four years ago this business lost money. Gross margin has since nearly doubled to about 20%, and GAAP operating margin turned positive in 2024. On the company’s adjusted measure, EBITDA margin was 11.3% in the second quarter, and it guides to 12 to 14% for 2026 and 20% by 2028.

Reported profit flatters the trend. 2025 net income included a $2.1 billion tax benefit, and the last twelve months include a $4.0 billion gain from revaluing the half of Prolec GE it already owned. Strip those out and earnings are far lower, which is why the headline P/E of about 25 is misleading.

Cash flow also runs ahead of profit, for a better reason: customers pay large deposits to secure turbine slots. Free cash flow was $5.1 billion in the second quarter alone, and 2026 guidance is $11.5 to $12.5 billion, more than double the earlier plan. That cash is real, but it is paid in advance for turbines still to be built, so it will slow as deliveries catch up with orders.

Read. Value the business on operating margins, not on this year’s net income or deposit-driven cash flow.

GE Vernova has net cash of about $10 billion, even after paying for Prolec GE, part of which was financed with $2.6 billion of senior notes. The plan is to return at least a third of the cash it generates. In the first half of 2026 it bought back 4.3 million shares at an average of $854 and returned $3.9 billion in total, and the quarterly dividend was doubled to $0.50 in December 2025.

The company also plans about $6 billion of capital spending and $5 billion of research and development from 2025 to 2028, much of it to lift turbine output from 20 to 30 GW a year inside existing factories.

Read. The balance sheet carries no risk to the thesis. The only real question is the price paid for buybacks: shares repurchased near $850 are worth it only if the growth case below plays out.

07 · What the price assumes

At $890, the market expects much more than the plan.

Running the model backwards shows what has to be true to justify $890.33. Holding everything else at the base case, the price needs one of these:

In practice the market is betting on a mix: higher prices for longer, margins above 20% after 2028, and a power cycle that lasts well into the 2030s. Peer multiples tell the same story. At about $227 billion of enterprise value, the stock trades near 20 times the 2028 EBITDA target and 38 times this year’s, multiples usually reserved for software companies.

The Street is more positive. The average of 35 analyst targets is about $1,172, with a range of $470 to $1,450, and most rate it Buy. Their targets rest on earnings well beyond 2028 at premium multiples.

Insight. The disagreement is about what happens after 2028. The company’s plan, taken at face value, supports roughly half the current price.

08 · Risks

What could go wrong, and what could go right.

RiskWhy it matters
The gas cycle turnsGE’s power business has boomed and collapsed before. If data-center demand slows or financing tightens, slot reservations are softer commitments than firm orders.
Execution on capacityRaising output from 20 to 30 GW while keeping quality and delivery dates is the core of the plan. Delays would push revenue and cash flow out.
WindLosses of about $400 million in 2026, unresolved claims at Vineyard Wind and a weak U.S. market could cost more than planned.
Tariffs and supply chainSection 232 tariff decisions and input costs could squeeze margins on fixed-price contracts.
ValuationAt about twice the base-case value, the stock is exposed to any disappointment in orders or margins, as the swing from a 52-week low of $530 shows.

Upside

Pricing keeps rising; electrification grows faster than planned on data-center and grid spending; nuclear (the BWRX-300) becomes a real business; and margins pass 20% as Wind shrinks. Any two together would move the value well above the bull case.

09 · Recommendation

Hold. Own the order book, don’t chase the price.

At $890.33 against a base-case value of $442 ($460 probability-weighted), the stock trades at roughly twice what the company’s own 2028 plan supports. The business is excellent: a sold-out turbine franchise, a growing grid business, net cash and rising margins. But the price already assumes the plan is beaten by a wide margin, so the call is Hold: keep an existing position, don’t add at this level.

What would change the view

Toward Buy: a raised 2028 margin target above 20%, gas pricing still climbing through 2027, or a pullback toward the bull-case value. Toward Sell: slot reservations failing to turn into firm orders, or the 2026 margin landing at the low end of 12 to 14%.

What to watch on October 28

Third-quarter orders, where Bank of America expects about $21 billion against a $22.2 billion consensus; gas gigawatts added toward the 125 GW goal; Power and Electrification margins against the 22% path; and any change to 2026 guidance.

Final view

A great business, priced for a greater one.

GE Vernova is one of the clearest beneficiaries of rising power demand. On the company’s own targets the shares are fully valued; the upside depends on what comes after 2028.
