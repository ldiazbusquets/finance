01 · The business

Space, communications and missiles.

L3Harris has three segments. Space & Mission Systems builds satellites, sensors and aircraft systems. Communications & Spectrum Dominance makes tactical radios and electronic warfare equipment, its most profitable unit. Missile Solutions makes solid rocket motors and propulsion for interceptors such as THAAD and PAC-3, a bottleneck for U.S. munitions supply.

In January 2026 the Pentagon committed a $1 billion investment in Missile Solutions ahead of a planned IPO of that business; L3Harris has since pushed the IPO to mid-2027.

Second-quarter revenue rose 8%, orders of $7.3 billion took the backlog to a record $42 billion, and Missile Solutions grew 14%. The company raised its 2026 revenue and EPS guidance and kept free cash flow at about $3.0 billion. Its 2028 framework targets about $27 billion of revenue and $3.5 billion of free cash flow.

The shares fell anyway, first on the IPO delay and debt, then in August when the board removed CEO Christopher Kubasik for a conduct violation and named insider Sam Mehta as his successor.

04 · Balance sheet

Leveraged, but deleveraging.

Debt is about $11.0 billion against $1.5 billion of cash, mostly from the Aerojet deal, with net interest of about $560 million a year. The company also received $973 million from the Pentagon’s preferred-stock investment in Missile Solutions, which the model treats like debt. It pays a $4.90 annual dividend and is buying back stock.

06 · What the price assumes

At $249, the market doubts the targets.

To reach today’s price, you needInstead of
Long-run cash flow of only about 9% of revenue14.6%, in line with guidance
Or a discount rate of about 10.9%7.9%

In other words, the price only makes sense if the 2028 cash targets are badly missed or defense is treated as being as risky as a tech stock.

07 · Risks

What could go wrong, and what could go right.

RiskWhy it matters
LeadershipA new CEO after a conduct-related exit, and shareholder law firms have opened investigations.
ExecutionAbout 60 new production buildings and a missile ramp through 2029; fixed-price contracts can bring overruns.
DebtInterest of about $560 million a year limits flexibility.
BudgetsDefense spending depends on Congress; the IPO depends on markets.

Upside: missile demand, space programs, and the Missile Solutions IPO putting a public value on the fastest-growing part of the company.

08 · Recommendation

Buy. A good business at a discounted price.

At $248.77 against a base case of $436 ($439 weighted), even a 9% discount rate gives $346. The selloff reflects real but temporary problems: a CEO change and a delayed IPO. The call is Buy, with the bear case ($231) close to today’s price as the main warning.

What would change the view: a cut to the $3.0 billion cash-flow guidance or new findings from the conduct investigation.

Final view

Headline risk, not business risk.

The backlog is at a record; the price reflects the boardroom, not the order book.
