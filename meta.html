01 · The business

Three billion people and an ad auction.

Meta earns nearly all its money from advertising on its Family of Apps. AI ranks what each person sees and which ads they are shown, and tools like Advantage+ let advertisers hand the whole campaign to Meta’s systems. Reality Labs, the headset and smart-glasses unit, loses about $4–5 billion a quarter.

The strategic bet is on building its own frontier AI models and the computing to run them, both to improve ads and to create new products such as Meta AI.

Takeaway. The ad machine is stronger than ever; the debate is about how much it costs to keep it ahead.

02 · Latest results

Revenue up 26%, a $9.9 billion legal bill.

Revenue grew 26% to $64.6 billion, about $1.2 billion more than analysts expected, with more ads and a 14% rise in the price per ad. Costs grew 68%, mostly because of a $9.89 billion charge for the settlement with state attorneys general over teen mental health, part of an agreement to pay up to about $18.6 billion. Operating income fell 37% to $13.0 billion; without the charge it would have been about $22.9 billion, a 35% margin. EPS was $3.83, or about $7.40 without the charge.

Capital spending of $34.9 billion was more than the $32.8 billion of operating cash flow, so free cash flow was negative $2.1 billion. Meta again bought back no stock. Guidance: fourth-quarter revenue of $73–76 billion, 2026 expenses raised to $175–179 billion to include the charge, and capex narrowed to $135–145 billion. There is no 2027 capex figure yet, but management expects strong revenue growth throughout 2027, continued heavy AI investment and Reality Labs losses near $15 billion. Meta also announced a stock dividend of one new non-voting Class C share per share held, in effect a 2-for-1 split, with the new shares expected to trade from January 19, 2027. The shares closed at $697.52 before the report and rose about 4% after hours.

04 · Balance sheet

From net cash to borrowing.

Meta had $82.5 billion of cash and securities against $80.9 billion of long-term debt at the end of September, so it is roughly net cash neutral after years of large net cash. Buybacks have stopped for now; the $2.10 annual dividend continues. The youth-safety settlement removes part of the legal risk management had warned about, but the cash has still to be paid and other cases remain open.

06 · What the price assumes

At $697, margins back near 50%.

To reach today’s price, you needInstead of
A long-run operating margin near 50%38%; Meta’s peak was in the low 40s
Revenue growth of about 21% a year for two years18% and 14%

Meta is closer to fair value than Amazon or Vertiv: a slightly lower discount rate or slightly faster growth closes most of the gap.

Upside: WhatsApp business messaging, AI agents that sell for advertisers, and spending easing after 2027 would lift free cash flow quickly.

08 · Recommendation

Hold. Strong ads, heavy bills.

At $697.52 against a base case of $531 ($551 weighted), Meta trades above the model at an 11% discount rate but close to fair at 9.5%. The quarter was better than feared: ads grew faster than expected and the legal charge is a one-off. But free cash flow is now negative and there is still no limit on 2027 spending. The call stays Hold. A move toward the mid $500s, or evidence that 2027 spending will fall, would make it a Buy.

Watch in the fourth-quarter report: revenue against $73–76 billion, the first 2027 capex guidance, when the settlement is paid, and whether buybacks resume.

Final view

The ad machine works. The AI bill is the question.

Meta can afford the spending; the price needs it to pay off.
