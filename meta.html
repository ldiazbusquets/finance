Meta Platforms

Meta owns Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Messenger, used by 3.6 billion people a day, and sells the ads in between. AI has made those ads work better and revenue is growing 28%. But Meta is spending so heavily on AI data centers that free cash flow fell to almost nothing last quarter.

01 · The business

Three billion people and an ad auction.

Meta earns nearly all its money from advertising on its Family of Apps. AI ranks what each person sees and which ads they are shown, and tools like Advantage+ let advertisers hand the whole campaign to Meta’s systems. Reality Labs, the headset and smart-glasses unit, loses about $4–5 billion a quarter.

The strategic bet is on building its own frontier AI models and the computing to run them, both to improve ads and to create new products such as Meta AI.

Takeaway. The ad machine is stronger than ever; the debate is about how much it costs to keep it ahead.

Revenue up 28%, free cash flow down 91%.

Revenue grew 28% to $60.8 billion, from both more ads and higher prices. Costs grew 55%, including $2.4 billion of legal charges and $1.2 billion of severance, so operating income fell 8% and EPS fell 13% to $6.18. Capital spending of $31.1 billion consumed almost all of the $31.9 billion of operating cash flow.

Meta issued about $25 billion of new long-term debt in the quarter and bought back no stock. The shares fell about 8% the next day. Guidance: third-quarter revenue of $61–64 billion, 2026 expenses of $165–169 billion and capex of $130–145 billion.

04 · Balance sheet

From net cash to borrowing.

Meta had $90.3 billion of cash and securities against $83.7 billion of long-term debt in June, so it is roughly net cash neutral after years of large net cash. Buybacks have stopped for now; the $2.10 annual dividend continues. Management also warned that youth-safety trials in 2026 “may ultimately result in a material loss.”

06 · What the price assumes

At $694, margins back near 50%.

To reach today’s price, you needInstead of
A long-run operating margin near 50%38%; Meta’s peak was in the low 40s
Revenue growth of about 21% a year for two years18% and 14%
A discount rate of about 9.4%11.2%

Meta is closer to fair value than Amazon or Vertiv: a slightly lower discount rate or slightly faster growth closes most of the gap.

07 · Risks

What could go wrong, and what could go right.

RiskWhy it matters
AI spending without returnCapex of $130–145 billion a year has to lift revenue or it simply lowers margins.
LegalYouth-safety trials, EU regulation and antitrust can cost money and limit ad targeting.
Reality LabsLosses of roughly $18–20 billion a year with no clear payoff date.
Ad cycleAdvertising is the first budget cut in a slowdown.

Upside: WhatsApp business messaging, AI agents that sell for advertisers, and spending easing after 2027 would lift free cash flow quickly.

08 · Recommendation

Hold. Strong ads, heavy bills.

At $694.25 against a base case of $516 ($535 weighted), Meta trades above the model at an 11% discount rate but close to fair at 9.5%. The call is Hold. A move toward the low $500s, or evidence that 2027 spending will fall, would make it a Buy.

Watch in the third-quarter report: revenue against $61–64 billion, any first view of 2027 capex, and whether buybacks resume.

Final view

The ad machine works. The AI bill is the question.

Meta can afford the spending; the price needs it to pay off.
