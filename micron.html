01 · The business

Memory, and the memory AI needs most.

Micron makes the two main kinds of memory. DRAM (73% of last quarter’s revenue) is the working memory in every computer, and includes HBM, the stacked memory that sits next to each AI accelerator. NAND (26%) stores data in phones, PCs and data center drives. Only three companies make DRAM at scale: Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron.

AI changed the industry in two ways. HBM uses about three times the silicon of ordinary DRAM for each bit, so making it takes capacity away from everything else; and AI servers need far more memory of every kind. The result is a shortage that Micron expects to be “much tighter” in fiscal 2027 and 2028 than in 2026.

Fiscal 2026 (to September 3) brought revenue of $133.2 billion, up 256%, and net income of $85 billion. In the fourth quarter DRAM prices rose in the high teens and NAND prices about 30% from the quarter before. For the first quarter of fiscal 2027 Micron guided to $61.5 billion of revenue, an 86.25% gross margin and $38.15 of adjusted earnings per share; it plans to step up buybacks from December.

Micron has also changed how it sells. It has signed 26 multi-year agreements with take-or-pay volumes, expected to cover more than 35% of revenue through 2030, with about $150 billion of remaining obligations at set prices and $32 billion of customer prepayments. More than 75% of 2027 output is already committed.

04 · Balance sheet and supply

$68 billion of net cash and new fabs from 2027.

Micron ended fiscal 2026 with $68.3 billion of net cash. Its new capacity arrives in steps: Idaho ID1 in mid-2027, ID2 and a Japan DRAM expansion in late 2028, a Singapore NAND plant in the second half of 2028 and the first New York fab in 2030. Samsung and SK Hynix are expanding too. Morningstar, which cut its fair value to $700, expects industry supply to roughly double by 2028 and prices to fall steeply from 2029.

08 · Rating

Buy. The best memory market ever, still a memory market.

Micron is executing well in the tightest memory market on record, and long-term agreements make this cycle steadier than past ones. The price assumes today’s profit lasts well beyond 2028.

What would change the value: The first signs of new supply, contract prices for 2027 and the December results would move the value most.

Ranges, not three cases. The expected value is the average of 4,000 runs of this report’s own model, with every input drawn from a range: the base case is the middle, and the bear and bull cases are the 10th and 90th percentiles. Business inputs move together; the discount rate moves on its own. The 25/50/25 weighting in the valuation section gives $717. How the ranges work

Two different questions. The rating is relative: it asks whether the stock is cheaper or dearer than the S&P 500 on the same assumptions. The absolute view asks whether it is worth its price at all: on its own the stock trades above our estimated value, which is 22% below the price.

On the same assumptions every report uses (a 5.15% Treasury yield and a 4.5% equity premium), the S&P 500 itself is worth about 68% of its price. The rating compares each stock with that: Buy if it is at least 15% cheaper than the market on this basis, Sell if at least 15% dearer, Hold in between. It is the same rule for every company, whoever owns it. The rating depends on that assumption: it would be Buy if the index grows 6% a year and Hold at 10%, against 8% here. How ratings work

Against the Street: Analysts average about $1,555 and use about 7 times next year’s earnings. This model instead values the whole cycle, with prices falling back to a mid-cycle margin of 35% from 2029, which is the main source of the gap.
