01 · The business

Office, Windows, and the cloud behind them.

Microsoft reports three segments. Productivity and Business Processes (42% of last quarter’s revenue) is Microsoft 365, LinkedIn and Dynamics. Intelligent Cloud (44%) is Azure, server software and GitHub. More Personal Computing (14%) is Windows, Xbox, Surface and search advertising.

AI runs through all three: Copilot is sold inside Microsoft 365 (more than 30 million paid seats), and Azure rents computing to companies building AI, including OpenAI, in which Microsoft holds about 27% after OpenAI’s 2025 restructuring.

In the quarter to June, revenue rose 18% to $90.0 billion and operating income 18% to $40.6 billion. Intelligent Cloud grew 32%, Productivity 14% and More Personal Computing fell 4%. Earnings of $4.81 a share included a $3.2 billion gain on Microsoft’s stake in Anthropic. For the full fiscal year revenue was $331.8 billion and operating income $155.2 billion.

Contracted backlog rose 84% to $678 billion, though only about 25% excluding OpenAI, so one customer drives most of it. Microsoft guided first-quarter Azure growth to about 45% and fiscal 2027 capital spending to $255–260 billion including finance leases, up from about $190 billion. The shares fell about 28% in the first half of 2026 and then rose 39% in the third quarter, the best quarter since 1991.

04 · Balance sheet and ownership

Net cash, and a quarter of OpenAI.

At the end of June Microsoft had $76.8 billion of cash and investments against $40.3 billion of debt. It owns about 27% of OpenAI on an as-converted basis, accounted for under the equity method, and has funded almost all of a $13 billion commitment. This model adds the stake at 27% of this site’s own OpenAI base-case value of $790 billion, about $213 billion (an estimate; OpenAI is private). It returned $10.2 billion through dividends and buybacks last quarter.

08 · Rating

Hold. The best software franchise, in its most expensive build.

Azure is growing above 40% and Copilot has more than 30 million paid seats, but capital spending of about $257 billion this fiscal year means free cash flow is growing far more slowly than profit. The OpenAI stake adds about $29 a share at this site’s own OpenAI value.

What would change the value: Azure growth against the 45% guided for the first quarter, the payback on the capex, and OpenAI’s share of the backlog are the next inputs.

Ranges, not three cases. The expected value is the average of 4,000 runs of this report’s own model, with every input drawn from a range: the base case is the middle, and the bear and bull cases are the 10th and 90th percentiles. Business inputs move together; the discount rate moves on its own. The 25/50/25 weighting in the valuation section gives $383. How the ranges work

Two different questions. The rating is relative: it asks whether the stock is cheaper or dearer than the S&P 500 on the same assumptions. The absolute view asks whether it is worth its price at all: on its own the stock trades above our estimated value, which is 28% below the price.

On the same assumptions every report uses (a 5.15% Treasury yield and a 4.5% equity premium), the S&P 500 itself is worth about 68% of its price. The rating compares each stock with that: Buy if it is at least 15% cheaper than the market on this basis, Sell if at least 15% dearer, Hold in between. It is the same rule for every company, whoever owns it. The rating depends on that assumption: it would be Buy if the index grows 6% a year and Hold at 10%, against 8% here. How ratings work

Against the Street: Analysts average about $559, a little above the price. The model’s lower value comes mostly from the net investment of $180 billion in fiscal 2027 and a 10% discount rate; at 8.5% it reaches $486.
