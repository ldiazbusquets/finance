Inflation, measured three ways

The U.S. publishes several inflation measures, and they rarely agree. The differences come from what each one counts and how it weights it, and they matter because the Fed targets one measure in particular.

The three measures

Why the measures differ

Weights: housing is roughly a third of CPI but a much smaller share of PCE, so rents move CPI more. Scope: PCE includes spending that households do not pay directly, like medical care paid by insurers. Formula: PCE adjusts as people switch to cheaper goods when prices rise, which CPI does more slowly. Over long periods those differences have left CPI running a few tenths of a point above PCE, though the gap can flip for months at a time when items weighted more heavily in PCE, such as health care, rise faster.

What the bond market expects

The 10-year breakeven, the gap between ordinary and inflation-protected Treasury yields, shows the average inflation investors are pricing for the next decade: —. When it rises, investors think inflation will last; when it stays near 2–2.5%, they trust the Fed to bring it back.

Takeaway. Watch core PCE for what the Fed will do, CPI for what households feel, and the breakeven for whether markets believe the Fed.
