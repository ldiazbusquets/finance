ASML

ASML, based in the Netherlands, is the only company in the world that makes extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines, without which no leading-edge chip can be made. 01 · The business

The only maker of the machine every chip needs.

Lithography machines print a chip’s circuits onto silicon wafers with light. ASML makes three kinds: EUV, which only it can build and which is needed for every leading-edge logic chip and, increasingly, for DRAM and HBM memory; older DUV machines, where it competes with Nikon and Canon; and High-NA EUV, the next generation, now being tested by chipmakers. About 28% of sales come from servicing and upgrading the machines already installed.

An EUV machine costs about $200 million and takes many months to build, so ASML’s sales follow its customers’ factory plans: TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SK Hynix and Micron. The AI build-out has pushed all of them to add leading-edge capacity at once.

Third-quarter sales rose 55% to €11.7 billion ($13.1 billion), with a record 57.5% gross margin and 121 machines sold, including 17 EUV. Net income of €5.0 billion included a €510 million gain on its stake in Mistral AI. Customer prepayments rose €5.7 billion in the quarter, the clearest sign of new orders now that ASML no longer reports bookings.

ASML guided to €14.2–15.2 billion of fourth-quarter sales and €44–45 billion for 2026, up 36%, at a 56% gross margin. It plans to raise EUV and DUV capacity by 30% in 2027 and says even that will not meet customer demand in 2027 or 2028; price increases should come through partly in 2027 and fully from 2028. China fell to 23% of machine sales from 42% a year earlier.

04 · Balance sheet and ownership

Net cash, and €12 billion of buybacks.

At the end of September ASML had €12.7 billion of cash and short-term investments against bonds of roughly €3 billion (an estimate; the latest balance sheet has the exact figure), giving about €9 billion ($10 billion) of net cash. It bought back €4.1 billion of stock in the quarter under a program of up to €12 billion running to 2028, and pays an interim dividend of €1.88 a share in November. Each ADR is one ordinary share; there are about 383 million.

06 · What the price assumes

At $1,561, 26% growth for two years.

To justify the price, you needAgainst
Sales growth of about 26% a year in 2027 and 2028, then the same fade22% and 15%
Or a long-run operating margin far above anything ASML has earned44%

ASML has never been cheap: its monopoly in EUV has earned it a premium for a decade. At about 25 times next year’s expected earnings the shares are pricing in something close to our bull case. The analysts’ average target is about $2,079. The shares fell about 7% today as Lam Research warned that clean-room space and parts will limit the whole chip-equipment industry into 2027.

08 · Rating

Hold. A monopoly, priced like one.

Every leading-edge chip runs through ASML’s machines, and demand is ahead of its capacity for at least two years. The price needs growth close to the model’s bull case.

What would change the value: EUV price increases from 2027, High-NA orders and the June 2027 investor day are the next inputs; a chip-equipment downturn is the main risk.

Ranges, not three cases. The expected value is the average of 4,000 runs of this report’s own model, with every input drawn from a range: the base case is the middle, and the bear and bull cases are the 10th and 90th percentiles. Business inputs move together; the discount rate moves on its own. The 25/50/25 weighting in the valuation section gives $1,085. How the ranges work

Two different questions. The rating is relative: it asks whether the stock is cheaper or dearer than the S&P 500 on the same assumptions. The absolute view asks whether it is worth its price at all: on its own the stock trades above our estimated value, which is 34% below the price.

On the same assumptions every report uses (a 5.15% Treasury yield and a 4.5% equity premium), the S&P 500 itself is worth about 68% of its price. The rating compares each stock with that: Buy if it is at least 15% cheaper than the market on this basis, Sell if at least 15% dearer, Hold in between. It is the same rule for every company, whoever owns it. The rating is the same whether the index is assumed to grow 6% or 10% a year. How ratings work

Against the Street: Analysts average about $2,079, assuming EUV price increases and capacity additions lift growth and margins well into the 2030s. The base case here has growth of 22% then 15% and a 44% margin, discounted at 11%.

Final view

A monopoly, priced like one.

The machines are irreplaceable; the shares already assume it.
