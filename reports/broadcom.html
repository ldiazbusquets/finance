01 · The business

The other way to build AI chips.

Broadcom has two segments. Semiconductor Solutions (about 70% of revenue) designs custom AI accelerators, which it calls XPUs, for companies that want an alternative to Nvidia: Google’s TPUs, Meta’s MTIA, and chips for Anthropic and OpenAI. It also makes the Ethernet switches (Tomahawk) and optical parts that connect tens of thousands of chips in a data center, plus older chips for phones, broadband and storage. Infrastructure Software is mainly VMware, bought in 2023, plus mainframe and security software.

Broadcom designs the chips; TSMC manufactures them. The business needs little capital of its own, which is why about 46% of revenue becomes free cash flow.

In the quarter to August 2, revenue rose 86% to $29.6 billion and AI semiconductor revenue 221% to $16.7 billion; software grew 29% to $8.8 billion. Broadcom guided fourth-quarter revenue to about $34.8 billion, with AI at $21.7 billion.

Management said it has secured supply for about $115 billion of AI revenue in fiscal 2027 and about $230 billion in 2028, across six custom-chip customers, and expects earnings above $30 a share in 2028. It named Anthropic as likely to be its largest custom-chip customer in 2027 and OpenAI the second. The gross margin is falling, to about 73% from 78%, because complete systems carry more memory bought from others.

04 · Balance sheet and ownership

Paying down VMware debt, and lending to customers.

At August 2 Broadcom had $24.0 billion of cash and $59.4 billion of debt, $35.4 billion of net debt left from the VMware purchase; it repaid $5.6 billion in the quarter and $1.5 billion more after it. It pays a $0.65 quarterly dividend.

A newer item: with Apollo and Blackstone, Broadcom set up a financing vehicle to help Anthropic and OpenAI pay for their chips. The first $35 billion closed in June for Anthropic’s first gigawatt. Broadcom says it may give “modest” guarantees on the hardware’s resale value but has not said how large they could become. In October chief executive Hock Tan said financing access and costs might become key limits on AI growth.

07 · Risks

What could go wrong, and what could go right.

RiskWhy it matters
Customer fundingAnthropic and OpenAI need outside money to buy their chips; Broadcom is now helping finance them.
ConcentrationA handful of customers make up most AI revenue; losing one design generation would show quickly.
CompetitionNvidia, and design houses in Taiwan, compete for the same custom-chip work.
SupplyLeading-edge wafers, substrates, HBM memory and lasers are all tight; Broadcom depends on TSMC.
MarginsComplete systems carry more bought-in memory, pulling the gross margin down.

Upside: the 2028 outlook delivered in full, more custom-chip customers, Ethernet winning inside AI racks, and VMware growing faster than expected.

08 · Rating

Buy. Custom AI chips, priced for half the plan.

Broadcom has secured supply for about $115 billion of AI revenue next year and says demand is higher. The price assumes about half the growth in its outlook, because two of its largest customers, Anthropic and OpenAI, need outside financing that Broadcom itself is now helping arrange.

What would change the value: Fourth-quarter results on December 9, the size of the guarantees in the financing vehicle and any change to the 2027 AI outlook are the next inputs.

Ranges, not three cases. The expected value is the average of 4,000 runs of this report’s own model, with every input drawn from a range: the base case is the middle, and the bear and bull cases are the 10th and 90th percentiles. Business inputs move together; the discount rate moves on its own. The 25/50/25 weighting in the valuation section gives $421. How the ranges work

Two different questions. The rating is relative: it asks whether the stock is cheaper or dearer than the S&P 500 on the same assumptions. The absolute view asks whether it is worth its price at all: on its own the stock trades 23% below our estimated value.

On the same assumptions every report uses (a 5.15% Treasury yield and a 4.5% equity premium), the S&P 500 itself is worth about 68% of its price. The rating compares each stock with that: Buy if it is at least 15% cheaper than the market on this basis, Sell if at least 15% dearer, Hold in between. It is the same rule for every company, whoever owns it. The rating is the same whether the index is assumed to grow 6% or 10% a year. How ratings work

Against the Street: Analysts average about $529, closer to management’s outlook. This model takes 2027 roughly as guided but haircuts 2028 by about a quarter and uses a discount rate of 10.7%.
