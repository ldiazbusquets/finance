01 · The business

Pure flash, made with Kioxia.

Sandisk sells only NAND flash. Edge (61% of last quarter’s revenue) is flash for phones and PCs; Datacenter (33%) is enterprise SSDs, now the fastest-growing part as AI systems store and move far more data; Consumer is memory cards and portable drives. It makes its chips in Japan through Flash Ventures, a joint venture with Kioxia that shares the cost of the factories.

Because it does nothing but flash, Sandisk is the most direct way to own the NAND cycle. That made it one of the best-performing stocks of the AI memory boom, and also the one most exposed if flash prices turn.

In the quarter to July 3, revenue rose 51% from the quarter before; about two-thirds of that came from higher prices and one-third from volume. The gross margin went from about 51% in the second quarter to 78% in the third and 84.6% in the fourth. For fiscal 2026 revenue was $20.2 billion, up 175%, and net income $11.4 billion.

For the first quarter of fiscal 2027 Sandisk guided to $10.3–10.8 billion of revenue, an 83–85% gross margin and $44–46 of adjusted earnings per share. It bought back $4.5 billion of stock in the quarter and the board added $14 billion to the buyback. Results come on October 29.

04 · Contracts and balance sheet

Price floors, and price ceilings.

Sandisk now sells much of its output under multi-year agreements whose prices can move only between a floor and a ceiling. Management expects them to cover over half of its bits in fiscal 2027 and about two-thirds in fiscal 2028. The floors protect it in a downturn; the ceilings cap how much it gains if the shortage worsens. The CEO has called a mid-80s gross margin a fair return, so today’s margin is close to the top.

At the end of fiscal 2026 Sandisk had $4.8 billion of cash and had repaid all its debt. The model assumes about 150 million shares by 2030, down from about 155 million, as buybacks continue.

07 · Risks

What could go wrong, and what could go right.

RiskWhy it matters
The flash cycleFlash prices drove almost all of the profit; new supply from 2028 could reverse them quickly.
Single productUnlike Micron, Sandisk has no DRAM or HBM to cushion a NAND downturn.
Contract ceilingsPrices are capped on more than half of its bits in fiscal 2027, limiting upside.
KioxiaIts factories are shared with Kioxia, so supply and costs depend on the joint venture.
VolatilityThe shares fell about 40% in July on AI overbuilding fears.

Upside: enterprise SSD demand from AI inference, high-bandwidth flash for AI systems, contract floors that soften the next downturn, and large buybacks.

08 · Rating

Sell. The purest bet on flash, at a peak-cycle price.

Sandisk turned a loss-making business into one with an 85% gross margin and no debt in about a year. The price sits close to the bull case, and its own contracts cap how far prices can rise.

What would change the value: First-quarter results on October 29 will show how far flash prices are still rising.
