01 · The business

Rockets, a satellite network and an AI cloud.

Space is the original business: Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy launches for NASA, the military and commercial customers, and Starship, the fully reusable rocket meant to cut launch costs by an order of magnitude. Connectivity is Starlink, internet from more than 9,000 satellites for homes, ships, airlines and phones. Super Intelligence (SI) came with the purchase of Elon Musk’s xAI in February 2026: the Grok models, the X platform, the Cursor coding tool, and data centers that lease computing to other AI labs.

SpaceX listed on Nasdaq as SPCX on June 12, 2026, at $135 a share, valuing it at about $1.77 trillion in the largest IPO on record.

Takeaway. You now own an AI infrastructure company with a launch business and a telecom network attached. AI is already more than half of revenue.

Revenue up 161%, and a first operating profit.

Third-quarter revenue of $14.6 billion beat estimates by about $1.7 billion. SI revenue was $7.6 billion, including $608 million from Cursor, as Anthropic, Google and others rented its computing. Starlink added 1.6 million subscribers to reach 13.6 million, fewer than analysts expected, but its operating profit nearly doubled to $2.4 billion. Rockets earned $1.8 billion and roughly broke even.

Net income was $4.4 billion ($0.35 a share). Management guided first-quarter 2027 revenue to $33–37 billion, more than double this quarter, as computing capacity grows from 1.7 GW to about 3 GW by year-end. Starship reached orbit on Flight 14 and deployed V3 Starlink satellites. The shares rose about 10% the next day.

04 · Balance sheet and ownership

$90 billion of cash, and Musk in control.

SpaceX held $89.9 billion of cash and short-term investments against $38.8 billion of debt and leases in September, thanks to the IPO. Analysts expect debt to rise sharply as the data-center build continues.

Musk owns about 42% of the shares but about 82% of the votes through ten-vote Class B shares, so public shareholders cannot change the strategy. Insiders are locked up for a year; other pre-IPO investors can start selling around December 8, which could add supply. Musk has also said a merger with Tesla is possible but not certain.

At $163, about $700 billion of revenue by 2030.

That sounds extreme, but management is guiding to a $140 billion annual pace by early 2027 and has talked about $500 billion to $1 trillion by the end of 2027 if its AI business scales. The price sits between the base and bull cases: it needs the AI buildout to keep paying back as fast as management says, which is under a year per data center today.

07 · Risks

What could go wrong, and what could go right.

RiskWhy it matters
AI demandMost growth is renting computing to AI labs. If their spending slows, SpaceX is left with expensive capacity and rising debt.
SpendingCapex of $70 billion or more a year means negative free cash flow for some time.
GovernanceMusk controls the vote; a Tesla merger or other related-party deals could be on his terms.
Lock-upPre-IPO investors can sell from early December.
ExecutionStarship, V3 satellites and new data centers all have to work on schedule.

Upside: Starlink Mobile (22 million monthly users), reusable Starship lowering costs for everything else, and AI revenue scaling as guided.

08 · Recommendation

Hold. A great asset at a full price.

At $162.57 against a base case of $118 ($138 weighted), the price already reaches toward the bull case. The business is extraordinary, but the valuation depends on AI growth that has only just started. The call is Hold, sized as a speculative position, with the December lock-up expiry as a possible better entry point.

Final view

Own the rocket company. Watch the AI bill.

SpaceX’s value now depends more on data centers than on rockets.
